One priced design for an opportunity: equipment, labor and costs in, retail price out. Internal only; the customer sees it through the proposal.

## Working on it

- The totals on the right update as you type, but nothing is kept until **Save build sheet**. Site layout, Proposal and Summary save first, then open.
- Each section folds from its blue header. Collapse all and Expand all are under the title.
- **Copy** makes another build sheet to try a different design; this one stays as it is.
- **Summary** puts the whole sheet on one page to read or print.

## The sections

- **Setup:** name, install type (limits racking and foundations to ones that fit), warranty program, internet, tax exempt, markup and commission.
- **Line items:** catalog items by category. Per-watt and per-module quantities fill in on their own. The pencil overrides a price; it needs a reason and is logged.
- **Arrays:** tilt, azimuth, panels and shading. Once the site layout has panels it is the master: arrays and module counts come from the drawing and are read-only here.
- **Production estimate:** Save and run production runs SAM and PVWatts (about 10 seconds). It lists what is still missing, and shows Out of date when the sheet changes.
- **Trenching and boring, Labor, Extras without markup, Contingency, Payment schedule, Warranty summary, Savings, Proposal, Internal notes.** Internal notes carry over to the project and never print.

## Prices and flags

- Each line keeps the price it was picked at. **Catalog price changed** (red): the catalog differs now; Copy at today's prices makes a repriced copy. **Check price** (orange): a price is older than the stale setting; confirm it in the catalog.
- Markup can't go below the floor on Sales rates. Commission is solved into the price, so the price covers it exactly.
- Contingency comes from rules on Sales rates (added up to a cap, with a minimum). Overriding it needs a reason.
- Gross margin leaves out extras without markup; the note under it gives the full formula.
